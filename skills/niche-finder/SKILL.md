# niche-finder

**An interview, not a document.** He does most of the talking. **One question at a time.**
Push back on vague answers. Do not move on until the current phase has a real answer.

**Output:** `brain/niche.md` — written only at the very end. **Expect 45–60 minutes;** say
so at the start.

**Read first:** `brain/ledger.md`, `brain/strategy.md`, `brain/psychology.md`.

**If `brain/ledger.md` is empty, say so and offer to do that first.** Phase 3 reads it. A
mission attached to a skill with no evidence produces content that over-promises, and an
engineering audience detects that instantly. He can proceed anyway — but knowing the cost.

Then tell him, in your own words:

> This produces a working answer, not a final one. It gets revised after real posts exist.
> A good-enough niche you can act on beats a perfect one you never reach.

---

## THE MODEL

```
MISSION  +  SKILL  =  DESIRED OUTCOME
```
**Mission** the constant — what you're actually for. **Skill** the vehicle, allowed to
change. **Outcome** what the two produce **for the audience.**

Two models not in use: *skill-only* — fast growth, trapped in one topic, cannot pivot
without losing the audience. *"You are the niche"* — needs an established presence and real
charisma; typical failure is one hit then churn.

---

## PHASE 1 — ANTI-VISION

**Do not start with what he wants.** Inverting is far easier, and he already knows it.

> "One paragraph. Not what you want — what you *don't* want your life to look like in ten
> years. Be specific and be uncomfortable. What quietly happens if nothing changes?"

**Push if generic.** "I don't want to be poor" is not an anti-vision. Look for the shape:
the comfortable job that takes forty years, the freelancing treadmill that became a second
employer, the skills that stayed theoretical, watching other people ship.

Then the **post-mortem:**
> "Assume that happened. Reverse-engineer it. What decisions caused it? What did you keep
> postponing? Where exactly did it go wrong?"

**The mission hides in the post-mortem.**

## PHASE 2 — MISSION

> "What's the opposite you're actually moving toward? Not a goal with a number on it. The
> thing underneath it."

Real: he'd care if the money worked out · it predates this project · it shows in decisions
already made. Fake: sounds like a headline · is a revenue figure · invented five minutes
ago.

One paragraph. **Call it the minimum viable mission and say so.**

## PHASE 3 — SKILL

> "What skill moves you *and other people* toward that mission?"

Then the hard constraint, and **do not skip it:**
> "Can you sell it today? Not learning-stage, not aspiration — has someone paid you, or
> could they this month?"

**Sellable today:** web development, SaaS/MVP builds. **Learning-stage:** AI features.
**Aspiration:** multi-agent automation, until a shipped client system exists.

Check against `brain/ledger.md`. If the mission needs a skill he doesn't have, say so and
ask which he wants to move: **the mission, or the timeline.**

## PHASE 4 — OUTCOME

> "Complete it: [mission] + [skill] = ___ for the person watching."

**From the audience's side**, never his. Not "I build credibility." What changes for them.

## PHASE 5 — AUDIENCE

Fails the same way every time.

> "Who exactly is this for?"

**If the answer is "everyone", "anyone interested in tech", or "developers and business
people" — stop and push back.** Content made for everyone gets a generic script and nobody
watches it.

Get concrete. Not "businesses" — **which**, what size, what stage, what are they trying to
ship, what have they tried and abandoned, **what do they type into Google at 11pm.**

Then: > **"The [what you do] for [who]."**

**Hesitation test** — read it to a real person in that group. **Hesitation = too broad.
Go narrower.**

## PHASE 6 — BULLSEYE

```
Ring 1  the exact niche      ← the positioning line
Ring 2  one step broader
Ring 3  broader still
Ring 4  broader still
Ring 5  the widest category  ← never make content here
```
**Mix 3-2-2** per seven pieces. Nothing at ring 5 — same cost to make, zero qualified
attention, and it poisons who shows up.

## PHASE 7 — THE NOT-LIST

> "What will you refuse to cover, even when it would get views?"

**A niche is defined by what it excludes.** Without this it drifts back to generic within a
month. **Minimum five entries, at least one should hurt.**

Non-negotiable: **anything about Capgemini work** · **anything not personally shipped.**

## PHASE 8 — THREE TESTS

**Report failures plainly rather than smoothing them.**

**1 · Hesitation.** Would a stranger in the group know instantly it's for them?

**2 · Buyer or peer.** Does this attract people who would **hire** him, or people who
**are** him? Peers are the supply side. Not an automatic fail — recorded, and the ratio
watched. **But if the answer is "almost entirely peers", say that clearly** and let him
decide with it in front of him.

**3 · Ninety days.** Could he produce this every week for three months without resenting
it? If not, the mission is borrowed.

---

## OUTPUT — `brain/niche.md`

Write **only after all eight phases.** Use his words.

```markdown
# niche.md
**Status:** WORKING — revise after 20 published pieces
**Locked:** <date>   **Method:** mission-based

## ANTI-VISION / ## POST-MORTEM / ## MISSION
## SKILL          Sellable today: <yes/no + evidence from ledger.md>
## DESIRED OUTCOME   <from the audience's side>
## POSITIONING    The <what> for <who>.   Hesitation test: <result>
## AUDIENCE       <what they're building, what they've tried, what they search at 11pm>
## THE BULLSEYE   1…5, mix 3-2-2
## THE NOT-LIST   Capgemini · not personally shipped · <three more>
## TEST RESULTS   Hesitation / Buyer-peer (recorded, not a gate) / Ninety days
## OPEN           <what's uncertain and what would settle it>
```

Then hand Shubham the revised block for the claude.ai project instructions.

## RULES
1. **One question at a time.** Eight questions gets eight shallow answers.
2. Push back on vague once, properly. Then take what he gives you.
3. **Never accept "everyone."**
4. **Never write his answers for him.** Offer examples of the *shape* of a good answer,
   never the content.
5. Report failed tests plainly. A hidden failure is not a legitimate outcome.
6. **Do not write `niche.md` early** — it makes him defend a draft instead of think.
