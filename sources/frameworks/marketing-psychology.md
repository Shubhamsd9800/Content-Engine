## Why nothing was installed

The repo is built for SaaS company marketing — search volume, a product team, an existing
funnel. Shubham is a solo builder at zero posts. Installing 50 skills is bloat wearing the
costume of capability.

What survives: **three named mechanisms that explain rules already in use.** A rule with a
mechanism can be argued with and adapted. A rule without one is superstition, and it breaks
the first time a situation doesn't match.

## The three

**ZEIGARNIK EFFECT** — interrupted or unfinished tasks stay in memory better than completed
ones.
→ The mechanism under **open loops and rehooking**, and a sharper statement of the
vending-machine problem: a piece that closes every loop inside itself is *forgotten*, not
merely dull. **Leave one thing unclosed.**

**PEAK-END RULE** — people judge an experience by its most intense moment and its ending,
not its average.
→ The mechanism under **the outro rule**, and why the last ten seconds carry
disproportionate weight for likes, shares and rewatches. It also implies something we were
not doing deliberately: **engineer one peak.** Uniform quality scores worse than an uneven
piece with a real high point.

**PRATFALL EFFECT** — a visible, non-fatal mistake increases likeability, **but only for
someone already perceived as competent.** For someone unestablished it inverts.
→ The mechanism under **failure posts** — and it carries a warning the rules did not state.
A mistake post from an unknown reads as incompetence, not humanity. **So a failure post
must carry the competence in the same piece:** root cause, fix, proof. Never the failure
alone, never as the punchline.

## Also useful

**Curse of knowledge** — you systematically overestimate what the reader knows. Fix: name
the thing a beginner would Google. **Mimetic desire** — people want what people they
identify with want; relevant to who you position beside. **IKEA effect** — people overvalue
what they helped build; relevant to CTAs that ask for something small.

## Searchable vs shareable

| | Targets | Tends to reach |
|---|---|---|
| **Searchable** | existing demand — "how to", "best X", "X vs Y", "what does X cost" | already looking → **buyer** |
| **Shareable** | created demand — novel insight, counterintuitive argument, original data | not looking → **peer** |

Catches a specific self-deception: **a run of pure shareable content is a run of peer
content wearing a more flattering name.** Shareable feels like the ambitious choice, and it
is the one that reliably fails to reach anyone who would hire you.

## Rejected from the same repo

**`social`** — conversational, asks the user five questions, no operating rules. Weaker
than `script-writer` and `linkedin-writer`.
**`offers`, `product-marketing`** — relevant when the offer matters. Not at zero posts.

## One caution

A catalog of forty biases is an invitation to justify anything after the fact. Use it to
explain a rule that already earned its place. **Never to invent a rule because a bias
exists that would permit it.**
