## ⚠️ THE FIRST THING, BECAUSE IT KILLS A HYPOTHESIS

Going in, the open question was: **is 34.7x the shape, or is it the comment-to-DM mechanic?**
On the best post the comments (13,293) outnumber the likes (6,334) more than two to one, and
`engagement = likes + comments`, so the CTA inflates the very number the ranking is built on.

**Answer: it is not the CTA.**

**All 10 of 10 posts run a comment-to-DM CTA.** Every winner, every miss. The worst post in the
set (0.18x) has one. A variable present in 100% of cases cannot explain the difference between
cases. The mechanic inflates the *scale* of every number here, but it does not explain the
*spread*, and the spread is what a teardown is for.

> **Carry this forward as a reading rule, not a finding about him:** when a whole account runs
> one CTA, its engagement numbers are comment-weighted end to end. Compare posts *within* the
> account freely. Never compare this account's raw engagement to an account that does not run it.

## ── STRUCTURES ──

### S1 · THE SELF-DEMONSTRATING ARTIFACT — used in 6 of 10 · **PROMOTE**

The post makes a claim about itself, and the post is the evidence for the claim. You cannot
scroll past without having already evaluated it, because the proof is the thing on your screen.

```
0-3s     Claim about THIS video, spoken flat, no setup.
         "This entire video was edited by Claude Code."
         On-screen text says the same words. Visual = the edit itself, doing the thing.
3-8s     Name the frame: which day, which style, what is being recreated.
8-20s    Point AT the artifact. "as you can tell, my face and my screen is on the bottom."
         The viewer is asked to inspect the evidence they are already looking at.
close    Comment-word CTA + a disappearance line (see S4).
```

**RETENTION**
- **5s** — the claim is unverified and the viewer is *inside* the test. They keep watching to
  judge it. This is retention by *jury duty*, not by curiosity.
- **12s** — he narrates the edit while the edit performs. Post 6: he says "my face and my screen
  is on the bottom" over a frame that is literally split that way. Post 7: he says "purple,
  gradient, ugly stuff" over floating purple gradient SaaS cards. **Spoken, on-screen text and
  visual carry the same statement in different words** — the triple hook, held for the whole body,
  not just the hook.
- **20s** — the **unreadable proof-of-work frame.** Post 5 puts a wall of raw Claude Code output
  on screen: bezier curves, pixel measurements, node chains. Nobody reads it. It is evidence by
  *density* — proof that a machine did something a human would not bother to.

**REWARD** — a checkable belief change: *this is possible, and the output is not slop.* Not a
method. The method is behind the DM. **The free piece sells the belief; the DM sells the how.**

**MECHANIC** — click confirmation, at maximum strength. The hook sets an expectation ("a machine
edited this") and the first seconds *exceed* it, because the viewer is watching the evidence.
`psychology.md`: *reality beats expectations → they stay.* Nothing else in this set does this.

**AWARENESS** — **broad.** "This video was edited by a machine" needs zero prior knowledge.
Not one winner opens at practitioner level.

**READER** — **buyer.** It opens at the cost end, not the craft end: stop paying editors, stop
testing editors, be the creative director. A peer watching learns a workflow exists; a buyer
watching learns their problem is solvable.

**DOWNGRADE** — at 1.5 years, no followers, zero clients: **it survives, and it is the single
strongest shape available to you**, because it needs no audience and no track record. The proof
is inside the artifact. *"This whole dashboard was built by Claude Code in one evening — here it
is running"* works at zero followers in a way *"here's why everyone is wrong about AI"* never can.
**What does NOT come with it:** his editing standard. If the artifact is not visibly good, the
mechanic inverts and the post becomes evidence *against* the claim. **The shape is only as strong
as the artifact you point it at.**

**TRANSFERS** — **yes.** Bar cleared: 6 of 10 and transfers.

**COUNT, honestly.** 6 of 10 posts make an artifact claim in the caption — posts 6, 7, 5, 8, 9, 10.
Only the 3 winners have transcripts, so the claim is counted at caption level for the other 7 and
the delivery is only verified on the 3. **The 4 posts with no artifact claim are 0.18x, 0.30x,
0.90x and 3.11x — three of the four worst in the set.**

### S2 · THE NUMBERED SERIES — used in 4 of 10 · does NOT promote · **WATCH**

"Day 1", "Day 4", "Day 5". A number in the caption implies the days before it exist and the day
after is coming. Zeigarnik: an unfinished sequence stays in memory.

**And it wires into the CTA.** The comment keyword *is* the day number — `comment "DAY 5"`. The
visible comments are people typing "Day 5", "Day 5", "Day 5". The series and the CTA are one
mechanism, not two.

**MECHANIC** — Zeigarnik / open loop across posts rather than inside one.
**READER** — both.
**AWARENESS** — broad.
**DOWNGRADE** — a series at zero followers has nobody who saw day 1. **It compounds; it does not
launch.** See the finding below — his own day one flopped.
**TRANSFERS** — **partly.** Worth running, but it cannot be the thing that carries the first posts.
**4 of 10 is below the 5-of-10 bar. Recorded, not promoted.** Re-check after the next scrape.

### S3 · PRE-EMPTIVE SELF-UNDERCUT — 1 of 3 winners · **noise, recorded only**

Post 7, second line: *"So if this feels like AI slop, I've probably shot myself in the foot."*

He names the viewer's objection before the viewer finishes forming it, and stakes his own
credibility on the answer. `psychology.md` rates objection handling the **highest-value hook
modifier** available, and this is a clean specimen of it.

**Once in three is noise by this file's own rule.** It is written down because it is the highest-
value modifier in the playbook and it appeared unprompted — not because the count supports it.

### S4 · THE DISAPPEARANCE CLOSE — 2 of 3 winners · below bar

- Post 6: *"Now I'm creating this releasing. So if you want to be in the loop, you might never see me again."*
- Post 5: *"Very soon, we might never see each other again. So if you want to see how far this gets, leave a follow."*

An unresolved threat as the last beat. Peak-end rule: the ending carries disproportionate weight,
and this ending refuses to close. It is a follow-driver disguised as a sign-off.

**DOWNGRADE — do not copy this one.** It works because he has something people want to keep
watching. At zero followers "you might never see me again" reads as nothing at all, because
nobody would notice.
**TRANSFERS — no.**

---

### S5 · COMMENT-TO-DM — 10 of 10 · clears the count, **fails transfer**

**MECHANIC** — a bounded, near-zero-cost action that converts a viewer into a DM thread.
**DOWNGRADE** — `psychology.md` is explicit: at zero followers this is shouting in an empty room,
and it signals desperation to the handful who see it. It also inflates engagement, which is
exactly why it is worth *understanding* and not yet worth *running*.
**TRANSFERS** — **no. Not yet.** Revisit at real follower numbers. Recorded so it is not
re-adopted by accident in three months.

## ── HOOK PATTERNS ──

| # | Opening line | Awareness | Mechanic |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | "This entire video was edited by Claude Code." | broad | claim-about-this-artifact |
| 7 | "This entire video was edited by Cloud Code. The captions, the cuts, the motion graphics, every single thing you see." | broad | same, then itemised — the list IS the proof |
| 5 | "This entire video was completely edited by Claude Code. I didn't put it there. Claude actually did." | broad | same, then the disclaimer-of-agency twist |

**Three winners, one hook.** Not a family of hooks — the *same sentence*, re-shot. Under 8 words
to the core claim in all three. No context, no setup, no "hey guys". `psychology.md`'s 4 S's are all
present in beat one: Subject, Stakes, Speed, Super-clear.

**The hook is not clever and it is not trying to be.** It is a flat factual claim that happens to
be surprising. **That is the whole trick, and cleverness would break it** — the claim has to sound
literal for the artifact to function as proof.

## ── PACING ──

From the frames, three per winner:

- **Layout, and it is consistent:** reference material / screen capture / diagram occupies the
  **upper two-thirds**, his talking head sits **bottom third**. Post 6 shows this explicitly —
  a hand-drawn `FORMATS × TOPICS` grid on graph paper up top, the reference creator's video
  mid-frame, his face at the bottom. **This is the layout Shubham identified independently.**
- **Text is kinetic and mixed-weight** — "if / this / feels like / **AI SLOP**" with the payoff
  word 3-4x the size of the setup words. Words land one at a time on the beat.
- **Two text zones per frame** — a large statement mid-screen and a small caption line at the
  bottom. They carry different halves of the same sentence.
- **Palette** — cream/white ground, black text, **one orange accent** for the payoff word. Post 6
  and post 5 both use the same orange. It reads as a brand, not a template.
- **Setting** — home, natural window light, cap and t-shirt. **Deliberately un-produced**, which
  is itself part of the argument: if the room is this casual, the polish came from the machine.

## ── LANGUAGE ──

**100% English. Zero Hinglish.** Casual, contraction-heavy, sentence fragments. One unbleeped
expletive in post 5. Sentences run 6-14 words. No jargon that needs decoding — "motion graphics",
"colour grading", "SFX" are named but never explained, and never load-bearing.

**Register: peer-to-peer, not teacher-to-student.** He never says "you should" except once, and
that once is in a *miss* (post 4: *"You should focus on recording and doing what you are…"*, 0.30x).

## ── HITS vs MISSES ──

Compared at caption level, because only the 3 winners have transcripts.

**Winners share:**
1. A claim about **this specific artifact** — not about his workflow in general.
2. A **series number** in the caption (3 of 3).
3. The caption carries **content**, not only a CTA.

**Misses share:**
1. **CTA-only captions.** The three worst posts in the set —
   `0.18x` *"comment BREAKDOWN to see how the workflow"*,
   `0.30x` *"Comment breakdown to edit with this pipeline"*,
   `0.90x` *"comment DAN and i'll send you the breakdown"*
   — are captions with **nothing in them but the ask.** No claim, no number, no reason to care.
2. Claims about **his workflow** rather than about the video: *"I don't edit my videos anymore"*
   (1.02x), *"this entire video cost me $600 to make"* (0.58x). A cost is not a demonstration.

> **THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST, AND IT IS UNCOMFORTABLE**
>
> **Post 8, 7 July: *"day one of letting claude code edit everyt…"* — 0.98x. The series launched
> to nothing.** Day 4 hit 13.85x six days later. Day 5 hit 7.58x.
>
> **The series did not work when it started. It worked once it had been running.** The open loop
> needs prior posts to be open. Anyone copying "start a numbered series" expecting day one to
> perform is copying the outcome, not the mechanism. **Days 2 and 3 are the price.**
