## ── SCRIPT #10 · 2.50x / 2.88x · ~190 words · his best ──

```
CLICK CONFIRM  "**Do you guys want to get better front-end designs with Claude Code?**
                Here are five plugins you need to install right now."
               confirms ✅ · beats ✗
               → **NAME IT: THE QUALIFYING QUESTION.** He opens by asking whether the viewer
                 wants the outcome. It is a lottery list compressed into one sentence — anyone
                 who answers yes has self-selected before the body starts.
               → **Cheaper than a lottery list and it does the same job**, but it filters
                 harder: one condition instead of five doors.

INTRO          context ✅ · common belief ✗ · contrarian ✗ · proof ✗
               plan ✅ "here are FIVE plugins" — the count, stated
               → TWO OF FIVE, and this is his ceiling. **He never argues, never contrasts,
                 never proves. He announces and enumerates.**

BODY           5 items, one sentence each, ordinal. FLAT.
               Each item: **name → what it does → the failure it prevents.**
                 "Taste Skill — pulls real design taste from premium references **so your UI
                  stops looking crappy and vibe-coded**"
                 "Web Design Guidelines — audits against Vercel's rules **and flags weak
                  points before you actually ship anything**"
                 "Playwright CLI — screenshots what it built **and catches crappy outputs and
                  errors before you see them**"
               → **NAME IT: THE FAILURE-AVOIDED CLAUSE.** Every item ends on a bad outcome
                 the reader recognises. Not "it does X" — "it does X **so Y doesn't happen to
                 you**." **A micro consequence chain, once per item.**
               → This is the CONSEQUENCE CHAIN (third account) miniaturised and repeated.
                 `devtalksbusiness` runs one chain for a whole script; **he runs five.**

VALUE LOOP     what 5/5 · how 0/5 · **why 5/5** — via the failure-avoided clause
REHOOKS        NONE.
OUTRO          the verbatim template close. No summary, no high note.
NATIVE EMBED   BOLTED ON.
E vs R         MET. The caption promised five plugins; five arrived.

WHAT TO STEAL  1. **THE FAILURE-AVOIDED CLAUSE.** End every list item on the bad thing it
                  prevents. **Turns a flat list into five small consequence chains, costs
                  six words each, and it is the cheapest `why` device found in 26 scripts.**
               2. **THE QUALIFYING QUESTION** as a one-line replacement for a lottery list.
WHAT TO DROP   The zero-proof intro. It works at his following; it does not at zero.
```
