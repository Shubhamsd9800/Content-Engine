## TRANSCRIPT

This Bengaluru engineer built an app that detects potholes, finds the responsible contractor, and files a complaint in four seconds. Here is how it works. This is my friend Gaurav Sen. He was driving to work in Bengaluru and hitting twelve potholes every day. Most people would just suffer, but he decided to hold people responsible. So he partnered with OpenAI and built an app with Codex. It not only detects potholes, it also finds who is responsible and makes a complaint. Here's how it works. He put a dash cam on his car with GPS and an accelerometer. This can tell when the car hits a bump and pauses, so the system records the road and detects potholes. The footage is then processed using AI vision models to identify potholes as small, medium, or large. Now, here's where it gets clever. Many Indian roads are still under construction warranty, so the contractor who built the road is actually responsible for fixing the potholes. Gaurav's app searches through two thousand nine hundred government contracts, finds the photograph, exact location, tender number, and the officer responsible for the road, all within four seconds. And then the app makes a structured record of this data, which is complaint-ready. So on just one drive to work, if you detect twelve potholes, you have twelve complaints ready by the end of your ride. Would you use this tool? Let me know in the comments.
