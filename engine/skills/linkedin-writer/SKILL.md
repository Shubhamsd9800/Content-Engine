**Read:** the Brief · `brain/niche.md` · `brain/voice.md` · `brain/psychology.md` ·
`brain/swipe.md`. **You never see the raw input.** If the Brief is thin, send it back to

**Write:** `work/drafts/<brief-id>-linkedin.md`
**Language: English. Always.**

---

## WHY THIS RUNS FIRST

~35 minutes end to end, against 2–3 hours for a reel once recording and editing count. So
the written version ships first and **tests the idea.** If it lands flat in text, an
afternoon of filming was saved. Only ideas that prove out in writing get recorded.

---

# STEP 1 — READ THE FOUR FIELDS THAT DECIDE THE POST

Before a single line is written, take these from the Brief. **They are decisions already
made. You do not re-decide them.**

| Field | What it changes |
|---|---|
| `READER` | **peer** or **buyer** — vocabulary, what you assume, what the close asks for |
| `RING` | 1, 2–3 or 4 — how specific the subject is |
| `FUNNEL SLOT` | TOFU / MOFU / BOFU — what the post is *for* |
| `STAGE` | found / installed / testing / shipped — the honesty level of every tool named |

**If `READER` says `mixed` or `unknown`, stop.** That Brief is not finished. Send it back.
A post written for both readers is a post for neither.

## READER — the register

| | **PEER** — students, engineers, working professionals | **BUYER** — small business owners, solo founders, no technical person |
|---|---|---|
| **Assume** | real tools, real versions, real file paths | nothing. They have never heard of an MCP |
| **Jargon** | free, unexplained | only named as a beginner would Google it, then one clause of explanation |
| **The unit of value** | the mechanism — *why* it broke | the consequence — what it cost in time or money |
| **Close** | what to try, or what to stop doing | what this means for the thing they are trying to get built |

**Explaining basics is not beneath this account.** Ring 2–3 is *the basics nobody is
teaching, for people who do not know the tools exist.* Writing over a buyer's head is the
failure, not the standard.

## STAGE — the honesty gate on every tool named

`found` haven't used it · `installed` haven't run it · `testing` running it, partial
results · `shipped` used it in something that exists.

**A stage may never be skipped upward.** The post must carry the stage in plain words.

> *"I installed it last week. Haven't run it yet."* — publishable at `installed`.
> *"Use this, it's excellent."* — requires `shipped`.

**And the line that must not blur:** the post shows what he **built.** It never implies
something was **delivered to a client.** Every ledger entry is `built-for-self` and there
are zero clients. Check the Brief's `STANDING TYPE`.

---

# STEP 2 — THE GATE

> **Reality must beat expectations.** If the post restates what the reader already knows,
> their existing knowledge IS the expectation and it cannot be beaten.

Check `GAPS` and `YOUR STANDING`. If both are thin this is a summary — send it back. Do not
write it well anyway.

**Then the slot, and it now branches by reader:**

| Slot | Opens on | Closes on |
|---|---|---|
| **TOFU** | a problem they already feel, or a position on the industry | a question from their experience |
| **MOFU** | what broke, or what you built | the mechanism, and what it cost |
| **BOFU — buyer** | what you build and who it is for | a plain statement of the offer. At zero followers this is a statement, never a pitch |
| **BOFU — peer** | **stop.** There is no product yet. A peer-BOFU is a hold until post 20. | — |

---

# STEP 3 — THE HOOK

First 2 lines, before the fold. Everything past line 2 is invisible until they click.

## Frame broad. Land narrow.

**The hook opens on pain or desire — not on the subject.** Some permutation of *"if you
want…"* / *"if you don't want…"*, or the cost stated flat.

> **Never open at the practitioner's level of awareness.** *"My chunk sizes were perfect,
> my retrieval was garbage"* filters to people already inside the problem — at zero
> followers that is almost nobody. Most people scrolling are not intentionally trying to
> learn. **Broad hook, narrow body.**

**4 S's:** Subject · Stakes · Speed (under ~10 words) · Super-clear.

**Mechanics — name the one you used:**

| Mechanic | Shape |
|---|---|
| **stated cost** | "I lost two days to a default I never questioned." |
| **desire/pain frame** | "If you don't want to spend a week on a landing page that still looks AI-generated —" |
| **the undercut** | state a result, then immediately undermine it |
| **contrarian contrast** | state the common belief, then contradict it |
| **objection handling** | "I know what you're thinking" then name their doubt |

---

# STEP 4 — THE BODY

**Story first, data last. This is the rule technical writers break.** Open on the problem
and what it cost. Numbers come *after* the story, supporting it. **Data → emotion → data**,
interleaved.

Three beats:
1. The problem, as it felt at the time
2. The common belief — what any competent person would have assumed
3. The contrast — what was actually true

Then the chain: **problem → root cause → fix → proof.** Structurally impossible to
summarise from someone else's video, which is why it builds a brand and a listicle does not.

**Don't summarise — zoom into the moment.** Location, actions, the raw unfiltered thought,
the exact words. "Two days of retrieval returning garbage" is a summary. The moment you
looked at the chunk boundaries is a scene.

**Named specifics beat generalities.** Real versions, real timings, real paths. "About two
days" beats a fabricated "16 hours" — and a real number beats both.

**Pratfall warning.** A failure post from someone not yet established reads as incompetence
unless the competence is in the same piece. **Root cause, fix and proof always travel with
the failure.** Never the failure alone, never as the punchline.

**When the source is someone else's story** — a founder's journey, a public failure — the
angle is **not** his reaction to it. **The angle is the reader's problem, told through it.**

> ✗ *"Sharan failed for two years and it's inspiring."*
> ✓ *"Sharan spent two years failing. You're eighteen months in. Here's the part of his road
> you're standing on right now."*

---

# STEP 5 — THE CLOSE

**At zero followers: conversation or return.** A question they can answer **from their own
experience** — not one testing whether they read the post.

- **To a peer:** *"What's a default in your stack you've never actually questioned?"*
- **To a buyer:** *"What did you try first, before you gave up on getting it built?"*

**Never "DM me."** Never a link in the body — first comment only.

# FORMAT

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | under ~1,300 characters for a text post |
| Line breaks | 1–3 line paragraphs. Dense blocks don't get read on mobile |
| Emoji | none unless asked |
| Hashtags | 3 max at the end, or none |
| Links | first comment, never the post body |

**Carousel** when the Brief has 4+ discrete steps or a before/after that needs showing. One
idea per slide, hook on slide 1, question on the last. State why carousel over text.

# RULES

1. **Never publish this draft.** Shubham always rewrites. This is a starting point.
2. **One post, one reader.** If you cannot name which, the Brief is not ready.
3. Story first, data last. Non-negotiable.
4. One idea per post.
5. **Never open at practitioner level.** Broad hook, narrow body.
6. **Every tool named carries its real stage, in the post.** Never skip a stage upward.
7. **Never imply a client delivery.** Built-for-self is the only standing that exists.
8. **Confidence downgrade** — "Here's why everyone is wrong about X" becomes "Here's what
   broke when I tried X."
9. Never claim what the ledger cannot back.
10. Banned openings and words: `brain/voice.md`. Hard list.
11. **If a draft could have been written by anyone about anything, it fails.** Delete and
    start again — do not polish it.
12. Log all 9 hooks in `work/log/hook-bank.md`.
