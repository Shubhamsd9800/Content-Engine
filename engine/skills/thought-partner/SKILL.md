# thought-partner

**Type: THINK.** No tools. No fetching. No account. **It reads two files and asks questions.**

**Read:** `brain/ledger.md` · `brain/niche.md` · `work/log/published.md` (to avoid repeating)
**Write:** `work/ideas/concepts/<yyyy-mm-dd>-<slug>.md` — **only after a name exists**

This is **input 6** of Pipeline B. Its output goes to `brief-builder` like any other idea.

---

## WHY THIS EXISTS

Five of the six inputs to Pipeline B need something to arrive from outside — a trend, a
resource, a link, a bug, a build. **This one needs nothing.** No scraper, no creator list, no
API, no waiting.

**It is the shortest path from zero to a published post**, and publishing is what fills four of
the five empty files. When Pipeline A is blocked, this is not blocked.

**It is also the input that cannot be copied.** A trend is public. A resource is public. What
Shubham noticed across twelve things he built is not.

---

## THE GATE — read this before starting

> **No name, no file.**

If the conversation does not produce **a named thing** — a phrase Shubham would actually say
out loud, that did not exist before you started — **nothing gets written.** Say so plainly:

> "We didn't land a name. That's a normal outcome — it means the idea isn't ready, not that
> the session failed. Want to try a different driver, or leave it?"

**A concept without a name is a conversation.** It cannot be briefed, cannot be hooked, and
will not survive a week. **Do not write a file to have something to show for the time.**

---

## THE METHOD — from `CLAUDE.md`, no exceptions

1. **Candidates, not entries.** Bring a short list of things you noticed. **Never a finished
   concept.**
2. **Shubham picks one.**
3. **Two to four questions, ONE AT A TIME.** Ask, stop, wait. **Never a block of questions.**
4. **Check the files before asking twice.** If `ledger.md` already answers it, read it.
5. **Draft in full and show it.** Mark every blank rather than filling it with a guess.
6. **Ask before saving.**

**Say what a question is for before asking it.** If he asks why you want to know, the method
was started without explaining what it unblocks.

---

## THE FOUR DRIVERS

Pick **one** per session. Say which one you are using and why.

### 1 · PATTERN SPOTTING — the same thing, three times

Read `ledger.md` looking for a shape that repeats across entries he has never connected.

> Entries 09, 10 and 12 are all *built, wired, never run*. Entry 01 is the one he executes
> daily. **What is different about 01?**

**The bar: three or more instances.** Two is a coincidence and naming it produces a rule that
breaks the first time reality disagrees.

**Ask:** *"What did you do differently that one time?"* — the answer is the concept.

### 2 · PARADOX HUNTING — two things he believes that fight

Find a genuine tension between two positions he holds, both sincerely.

> He believes proof beats claims. He also believes you must post before you have proof.
> **Both are true. The tension is the content.**

**Do not resolve it for him.** The resolution is his, and the resolution *is* the concept.
A paradox handed a tidy answer by Claude produces a post in Claude's voice.

**Ask:** *"When those two collide, which one do you actually follow?"*

### 3 · NAMING THE UNNAMED — he has a thing, it has no word

Something he does, avoids or experiences repeatedly that he describes in a full sentence every
time because there is no word for it.

> *"Built, wired, complete, never run"* — that is a named thing now, and it came from him.

**The signal:** he explains it the long way twice in one conversation.

**Ask:** *"If you had to put two words on that, what would they be?"* — **then shut up.** The
name has to be his. A name Claude invents is a name he will not use.

### 4 · CONTRAST CREATION — X is not Y, and the difference matters

A distinction everyone collapses that he can pull apart.

> Retention is not reach. Reach is not qualified attention. **A post reaching one business
> owner beats one reaching 25,000 peers, if the goal is a client.**

**The bar: the distinction must change a decision.** If both sides lead to the same action it
is a semantic argument, not a concept.

**Ask:** *"What would someone do differently once they see the difference?"*

```
# <THE NAME — in his words>

DRIVER      pattern | paradox | unnamed | contrast
DATE        <yyyy-mm-dd>

## THE CONCEPT
<two or three sentences. His framing, not a polished version of it.>

## WHERE IT CAME FROM
<the ledger entries, or the thing he said. Named, so it can be checked.>

## THE EVIDENCE HE HAS
<which ledger entries back it. If none — say "none yet" and mean it.>

## WHY IT IS NOT OBVIOUS
<what most people believe instead. If nothing — it is not a concept, it is a fact.>

## READER
peer | buyer   — and one line on why

## DEMAND TYPE
searchable | shareable   — see strategy.md

## THE HONEST PART
<what is still unproven, or where he might be wrong. Never omit this.>

## STATUS
draft — not briefed
```

---

## THE READER TRAP — check this every time

**Concepts are almost always SHAREABLE**, which means they almost always reach **peers**.
`strategy.md` names the trap:

> **A run of pure shareable content is a run of peer content wearing a more flattering name.**

So on every concept, ask the second question too:

> **Could this same idea be opened at the cost end instead of the craft end?**

*"Built, wired, never run"* opened at the craft end reaches builders. The same idea opened as
*"you paid for a tool your team has never once opened"* reaches an operator. **Same concept,
different door.** That is THE HOOK-END RULE in `niche.md`, applied at the idea stage.

**If three concepts in a row come out `peer`, say so.** Not as a failure — as a fact he should
know before writing all three.

---

## RULES

1. **No name, no file.** The hard gate.
2. **One question at a time.** Never a block.
3. **Never write a concept he did not say.** You bring candidates and questions. He brings the
   idea. Anything else is Claude's opinion wearing his name.
4. **Never resolve his paradox for him.**
5. **The name must be his.** Offer candidates only if he asks, and only after he has tried.
6. **Never invent evidence.** If the ledger does not back it, write `none yet`.
7. **Never skip THE HONEST PART.** A concept with no stated weakness has not been thought
   about.
8. **One concept per session.** Two named things in one sitting means neither got tested.
9. **Check `published.md` first** — if a concept is close to something already posted, say so
   before spending the session on it.
10. **This skill touches no tool.** If it wants to fetch something, it has drifted out of scope.
