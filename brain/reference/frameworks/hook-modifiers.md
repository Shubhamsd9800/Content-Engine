## The distinction this source owns

Every other source teaches what the hook **says**. These are things layered **on top of a
hook that already works.** Different layer.

> **A modifier cannot rescue a weak hook.** A summary of common knowledge with a countdown
> timer on it is a summary of common knowledge with a countdown timer.

The mechanism, stated by the source:

> **The number one reason someone scrolls off is that they subconsciously deem you not
> worth their time.**

That reframes the hook's job. Not "be interesting" — **produce evidence, in under three
seconds, that the next forty seconds are worth it.** Every modifier is a cheap way to
produce that evidence.

## The six

| # | Modifier | How it works | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"In [n] seconds"** | a stated time bound — an open-ended ask becomes a bounded one | **yes.** Works with a technical claim: "the whole fix, in 40 seconds." |
| 2 | **On-screen countdown** | makes the bound visual; the falling number is itself an open loop | **yes, sparingly.** Once it's a signature it stops interrupting. |
| 3 | **Visual / numerical social proof** | answers "worth my time" with evidence rather than assertion | **not available.** There is no real number, and manufacturing the appearance of one is the confidence-downgrade violation. Unlocks when a number exists. |
| 4 | **Objection handling** — *"I know you're probably thinking…"* then name their doubt | pre-empts the exact thought that causes the scroll. Being understood is itself proof you know the domain. | **yes — the strongest of the six.** Built for an unusual technical claim: "I know what you're thinking — chunk size was fine. It was." |
| 5 | **Physical open loop** — start a task you finish by the end (Lego, cutting fruit) | retention comes from the task, independent of the point | **not used.** A gimmick cannot carry a technical claim from someone 1.5 years in. |
| 6 | **Beat drop in the first 15s**, on the reveal line | audio punctuation marking the payoff as a payoff | **yes.** Free, invisible, purely an edit decision. Put it on the root-cause line. |

## How they get used

1. Write the hook with the content rules — 4 S's, triple hook, contrarian contrast, click
   confirmation.
2. **Then** ask whether one modifier makes committing cheaper.
3. **Never more than two on one hook.** Three is a magic trick and signals thin content.
4. No modifier fitting naturally is the common case. Ship without one.

## Conflicts

**Modifier 3 vs confidence downgrade.** Our rule: authority does not transfer and must not
be implied. **The modifier stays locked** — not softened, locked.

**Agrees with Kallaway** that the first three seconds carry the whole load and that the
mechanism is involuntary rather than persuasive.
