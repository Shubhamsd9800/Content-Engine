## STEP 1 · Optimise the profile before publishing

> *"Driving traffic to a profile that is not optimized is a great way to waste a lot of time
> and effort."*

The profile must answer three things: **who you help · what result you create · why they
should trust you.** Not a sales page. Just clear.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Picture** | In focus. Face clear. Recent. Looks like you. Professional enough for the people you want to reach. Nothing more. |
| **Banner** | Two elements only — **the benefit you offer**, and **social proof supporting it**. Benefit on one line; logos, results, investors below. Right-align the important content and leave padding so the profile picture does not cover it on mobile. |
| **Headline** | Three parts, in order — **the outcome you help create · social proof · your title.** No buzzwords. Understood on one read. Also seen every time you comment on someone else's post. |
| **Featured** | Three slots, used as a **proof library**: one strong case study · your best or most useful post · a notable press mention, podcast, guide or customer result. |
| **About** | Six beats: who you help · the problem you solve · the effect of that problem · the solution you offer · proof · the next step. |
| **Experience** | Correct title, plain explanation of what the company does. No fluff. Someone who scrolled this far wants answers. |

**Profile checklist `[DOC]`**

```
☐ Picture is clear and recent
☐ Banner explains the benefit offered
☐ Headline states an outcome, proof, and title
☐ Featured section contains useful proof
☐ About section explains who I help and how
☐ Experience section explains what the company does
☐ A COLD BUYER can understand the profile without extra context
```

## STEP 2 · Start with the questions your buyers want answered

**The failure this replaces, stated exactly:** people ask an AI tool for *"50 LinkedIn post
ideas for a founder"*, get a list that could apply to any founder in any market, write a
generic post, and get nothing from it.

> **A strong content idea sits between two things:**
> **1 · something your buyer already wants to understand**
> **2 · something you are qualified to explain**
>
> *"A buyer question without your experience produces generic advice. Your experience without
> a buyer question can produce a post that nobody needs."*

### Where he says the questions live

Sales call transcripts · customer calls · onboarding calls · support tickets · common
objections · FAQs · product feedback · lost deal notes · customer results · internal
documents · processes your team uses · mistakes you have made · decisions you have changed
your mind about · strong opinions you can support.

**Sales calls first**, because buyers state their goals, pain points, risks, objections and
failed solutions *in their own words*.

### His prompt, verbatim `[DOC]`

> *Analyze transcripts from our last 50 or more sales calls and identify the recurring goals,
> pain points, risks, objections, failed solutions, and questions raised by our target
> buyers. Use their exact language wherever possible. Turn those patterns into content topics
> for a cold audience. Start from the buyer's current situation and show them how to reach
> their desired future state. Do not assume they already understand our solution.*

**And immediately after it, the line that matters most:**

> *"The output is a starting point. You still need to add the part only you can provide.
> You must write sh\*t Claude cannot."*

### The four-question test before an idea enters the calendar `[DOC]`

```
1  Does my buyer already care about this question?
2  Do I have something useful and specific to say about it?
3  Can I support the answer with a real example, result, process, or opinion?
4  Will the answer help the reader make a decision or get a result?
```

Fail any one → keep digging. Review another call, ask a customer, interview the founder.
**Never fill the gap with a made-up claim.**

### The buyer-question map — four columns `[DOC]`

```
BUYER QUESTION  |  EXACT BUYER LANGUAGE  |  ANSWER AND PROOF  |  POSSIBLE POST
```

Keep their language. It improves topics, hooks, examples and sales copy. Target: **15–30 real
questions** before moving on.

## STEP 3 · Map top, middle and bottom-of-funnel buckets

A **content bucket** is a repeatable subject area that can produce many posts.
One bucket → several subtopics → several buyer questions → several posts per question, when
the angle, example, format or result differs.

### TOP OF FUNNEL — get visibility

Broader than the product. Company building, team building, personal experience, timely
conversations.

Company-building stories · company-building opinions · team-building opinions · hiring and
firing lessons · team updates, hires and promotions · fundraising lessons · personal stories
with a clear takeaway · **timely industry topics**

### MIDDLE OF FUNNEL — build trust

Answers the questions buyers actually ask. Shows how you think and why your experience
matters.

Frameworks for getting the result the buyer wants · processes your team uses · common
mistakes and their effects · industry opinions · commentary on market changes · breakdowns of
successful work · lessons from customer calls · product or operational decisions · tradeoffs
buyers need to understand

> **Every MOF post needs a point of view, framework, result, process, example or lived
> experience.** Supplement with industry commentary — tweet screenshots, post screenshots,
> headline reactions — **when you have something useful to add.**

### BOTTOM OF FUNNEL — convert trust

Case studies · testimonials · answers to common FAQs · answers to common objections · product
updates · feature explanations · company announcements

**The reach note, and it is important `[DOC]`:**

> *"A case study with 2,000 impressions may help a buyer trust you enough to book a call. A
> broad company-building post with 100,000 impressions may create no direct pipeline. Both
> can still do their jobs."*

**Reach alone does not decide the value of a post. Judge each post by the job it was created
to do.**

## STEP 4 · Build a monthly calendar

**12–20 posts per month = 3–5 per week.**

| Mix | Shape | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Preferred** | 2 TOF · 2 MOF · 1 BOF | default |
| Alternative | 1 TOF · 3 MOF · 1 BOF | more room to answer buyer questions and show expertise |

An account with little reach may need more TOF stories and opinions. An account already
reaching the right people may need more MOF and BOF.

### Ten fields per calendar row `[DOC]`

```
publishing date · funnel stage · content bucket · buyer question · post idea
source material · your proof or experience · PURPOSE · possible package/format · status
```

### PURPOSE must have a literal answer

Useful purposes: get visibility from a broader founder audience · build trust with Heads of
Marketing · explain a process buyers ask about on sales calls · handle a common objection ·
show proof through a customer result · share a founder story · create discussion around a
real opinion.

**Banned as a purpose: "build thought leadership."** Write down what the post should *do*.

### His four-week example `[DOC]`

| Day | Stage | Idea | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | TOF | A company-building mistake I made during our first year | Help founders know me and learn from the story |
| Tue | MOF | How we turn one 45-minute founder interview into 10 posts | Explain the process and reduce a workload objection |
| Wed | TOF | My opinion on founders using AI to write every post | Create discussion around a real belief |
| Thu | MOF | The five questions we ask before writing a LinkedIn post | Teach a useful framework and show expertise |
| Fri | BOF | How a client generated $65,000 in attributable revenue in 60 days | Show proof and explain what the work involved |

Build a few weeks ahead. Change it when a timely topic appears. **The point is to remove the
daily question of what to post.**

# PART 2 · CPIO — the writing framework `[DOC]` `[R1]`

**C**onvey · **P**ackage · **I**nformation · **O**rder.

> **Complete the four steps in sequence. Do not draft until each step is clear.**
> *"After and only after CPIO is complete should you write."*

He runs a **CPIO worksheet for every post.** Not in his head. Written down.

---

## C · CONVEY — what the post must communicate

> *"Your post should convey ONE thing. Nothing more. Every sentence and argument in the post
> should serve that point. This is how you write cohesively."*

Is it a story? An opinion? A framework? A process? A case study? `[R1]`

**Three questions:**
```
1  What should the reader understand after reading this post?
2  What part of the source material supports that idea?
3  What result should the post create?
```

**Question 3 needs a literal answer** — build trust, show expertise, share a founder story,
explain a process, create discussion around a real opinion.

> *"If you cannot state the purpose in one clear sentence, review the source material again.
> Do not invent a stronger opinion, lesson, story, or conclusion than the source supports."*

**Output:**
```
Convey: [one sentence stating the exact purpose of the post]
```

---

## P · PACKAGE — how the idea is presented

`[R1]` calls this **"arguably the most important"** of the four.

Packaging controls **angle, format, hook and structure.**

### The package decision table `[DOC]` `[R1]`

| The idea is | The package |
|---|---|
| **A story** | Story hook + founder picture |
| **A framework** | Desired-result hook + a useful worksheet or visual |
| **A long list of useful points** | Carousel |
| **Industry commentary** | Headline, tweet or post screenshot + your reaction |
| **A process** | Result-led hook + a clear breakdown of the steps |
| **A case study** | Result → starting point → process → proof |
| **A dense process** `[R1]` | Infographic or cheat sheet |

> *"Do not force a format because it is familiar or easy to write."*
> *"You'll build a muscle for this over time."* `[R1]`

### The hook — create a curiosity gap

> **A curiosity gap is the space between what the reader now knows and what they need to keep
> reading to learn.** `[DOC]` `[R2]`

The hook has one job: **give the right reader a reason to press "see more."**

Give enough information to understand the value. **Keep the useful answer for the body.**

> **The hook makes a promise. The rest of the post must pay it off.** If it does not, you
> lose the reader.

**Eight elements strong hooks use `[DOC]`** — one or more:
```
1  A specific result
2  Relevant authority
3  A clear desired outcome
4  A real problem
5  The start of a story
6  A process the reader wants to understand
7  A strong opinion you can support
8  A useful piece of information left unresolved
```

**Three curiosity-gap mechanisms, with his examples `[R2]`:**

| # | Mechanism | His example |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Pair two things that shouldn't go together** | *"I run a 100 person marketing agency, but I've never had a marketing job."* |
| **2** | **Reveal the decision, hold back the reason** | *"Last month, I turned down the biggest client our agency had ever been offered."* |
| **3** | **Start at the moment something changed** | *"Ten minutes into my first board meeting, the CEO closed his laptop and asked everyone to leave."* |

**On specificity `[DOC]`:**
> *"You could say you have written a lot of posts for venture-backed founders. Saying you have
> written more than 5,000 posts for more than 50 founders and executives backed by YC, a16z,
> Accel, and more gives the reader useful detail."*

**Three hard rules on hooks:**
```
✗  Never invent a number, result, time frame or claim because it would improve the hook
✗  Do not create false suspense
✗  Do not reveal the full insight in the opening
```

### Media — part of the package

| Use | When |
|---|---|
| **Founder photo** | personal story, vulnerable post, founder experience |
| **Internal doc / screenshot / visual** | framework, process, checklist, result |
| **Video** | when hearing the founder explain it builds trust |
| **Carousel** | a long sequence or list that benefits from pages |
| **Headline or post screenshot** | industry commentary |
| **Text alone** | when no media improves the idea |

> **"Media can improve a strong idea. It cannot rescue a weak one."**

**Output:**
```
Package:      [the chosen method or format]
Angle:        [how the idea will be presented]
Hook posture: [what the hook leads with, and what stays unresolved]
Media:        [what media, if any]
```

## I · INFORMATION — what goes in, and what does not

> *"What information needs to be in the post to make your point? Also, very importantly, what
> does NOT need to be in the post to make your point?"* `[R1]`

Do not include every detail from the source. Choose what improves **the argument, the story,
clarity, evidence, or reader value.**

> **"Every sentence fights for its life and earns its place."**
> Aim for high information density — the full idea in the fewest words that preserve its
> meaning and value.

**His worked example — what this guide itself required `[DOC]`:**
```
· social proof showing he is qualified to teach the process
· the blank-page problem that appears on sales calls
· the buyer-question method for finding topics
· content buckets for organising topics
· a monthly publishing schedule
· the CPIO framework
· the editing checklist
· the monthly review process
```

**And what he cut, with the reason:** DMs, comments, profile design, Sales Navigator — related,
but *"they do not belong in a focused writing guide because they weaken the main point."*

**Three accuracy rules:**
```
· Verify every specific claim
· Never fabricate statistics, dates, revenue figures, time frames or performance metrics
· If the source gives an approximate figure, KEEP IT APPROXIMATE
· If required information is missing, FLAG IT. Do not guess.
```

**Output:**
```
Required information: [facts, stories, examples, context the post needs]
Exclude:              [related source material that would weaken the focus]
Missing information:  [what is needed to write accurately — "None" when complete]
```

## O · ORDER — how the information flows

> *"How can you order it in the clearest and most compelling way for a COLD READER who does
> not know who you are, what you do, or how you can help them?"*

**His worked example — the order of this very guide `[DOC]`:**
```
1  Promise the desired outcome
2  Give social proof that establishes competence
3  Name the blank-page problem
4  Find questions buyers want answered
5  Organise the questions into content buckets
6  Build the calendar
7  Use CPIO to write each post
8  Edit the post
9  Publish consistently
10 Review performance and improve
```

> *"This follows the order in which the reader encounters the problems."*

**The five structural roles:**

| Role | Job |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | establish value, create enough interest to continue |
| **Setup** | give the reader enough context to understand the topic |
| **Development** | develop the idea in the order that makes it easiest to understand |
| **Support** | evidence and examples, placed where they strengthen the point being made |
| **Ending** | complete the idea naturally |

**Three closing rules:**
```
✗  Do not repeat the conclusion in different words
✗  Do not add a CTA unless the source, strategy or brief calls for one
✓  The ending completes the idea. It does not summarise it.
```

**The self-check `[R1]`:**
> *"Read the post as a third-party viewer after writing it and see if it makes logical sense.
> Or tell ChatGPT or Claude to act as a COLD READER and see what it thinks."*

**Output:**
```
Hook:        [what the opening must establish]
Setup:       [what context comes next]
Development: [how the main idea will develop]
Support:     [where examples, evidence and details belong]
Ending:      [how the post should finish]
```

# PART 3 · THBM — the four levers `[R2]`

**T**opic · **H**ook · **B**ody · **M**edia.

**This is not a second framework.** It is the same system described from the output end rather
than the process end. Recorded so the mapping is on file and nobody rebuilds it twice.

```
CPIO — the decisions before drafting     THBM — the parts of the finished post

C  Convey                            →   T  Topic
P  Package                           →   H  Hook  +  M  Media
I  Information  +  O  Order          →   B  Body
```

**T · TOPIC** — *"must make the reader know, like, or trust you better."*
```
KNOW + LIKE   →  stories and opinions
LIKE + TRUST  →  frameworks, processes, case studies
```
*"Your post should aim to convey one thing. Every line should support that point.
**If it does not support the point, cut it.**"*

**H · HOOK** — *"the most important sentence you will write in the post."* Covered above.

**B · BODY — three rules `[R2]`**
```
1  Write only as long as the post needs to be. Every line fights for its life.
2  Don't overdo or underdo line breaks.
   USE THEM TO SEPARATE IDEAS AND ARGUMENTS — NOT INDIVIDUAL LINES.
3  Order the points and arguments the way they make most sense TO A COLD READER
   who does not know who you are.
```

**M · MEDIA** — covered in Package above.

# PART 4 · STEP 6 · Drafting in simple language `[DOC]`

**Write for a cold audience.**
> *"The algorithm serves your content to people sporadically. Give the reader the context they
> need each time."*
Not your life story every post. Just enough to understand why the idea matters and why you
are qualified to share it.

**Write how you speak.**
> *"If you would not say 'utilize' when speaking to someone, do not write it. Say 'use.'"*
Shortest, simplest word that communicates the idea. **Read the post aloud before publishing.
If a sentence sounds awkward spoken, rewrite it.**

**Clarity beats cleverness.**
> *"Your reader is not stupid, but you should write like they are."*
Simple language, easy analogies, active voice where practical — *"we built this feature"* not
*"this feature was built by us."* One main idea per sentence.

**Make every sentence flow.**
Check how each sentence connects to the next. If you must reread to see the connection, so
will they.
> ***"Do not use repeated openings, stacked fragments, or dramatic cadence to fake momentum.
> The logic should create the momentum."***

**Format for mobile.**
```
· You do NOT need a line break after every sentence
· Avoid large blocks of text
· Line breaks separate IDEAS
· Bullets or numbered lists when they help the reader hold several distinct points
· Avoid blocks spanning more than FOUR LINES on a typical mobile screen
· Do NOT use special characters to fake bold or underlined LinkedIn text
```

**Cut everything that does not matter.**
Remove *very*, *really*, *quite* when they add nothing. Remove any sentence that does not
teach, support the point, improve the story, or set up what comes next.
> *"A post can be long when the idea needs detail. Do not make it longer than the idea
> requires."*

# PART 5 · STEP 7 · THE EDITING CHECKLIST — the gate `[DOC]`

> *"Do not skip this. I hate it as much as the next person, but trust me, you need it."*

Run **every** post through it before publishing. Six groups, 29 checks.

### Point and Payoff
```
☐ The post makes one clear point
☐ The body fully pays off the promise made in the hook
☐ Every part of the post supports the original CONVEY goal
☐ The ending completes the idea without repeating it
```

### Context and Usefulness
```
☐ A cold reader has the context needed to understand the post
☐ The post gives the reader a useful takeaway
☐ The examples and explanations are specific enough to use
☐ Related details that weaken the focus have been removed
```

### Hook and Package
```
☐ The hook gives the right reader a clear reason to press "see more"
☐ The hook creates honest interest in the source idea
☐ The format and media fit the idea
☐ The hook contains no invented number, result or claim
☐ The post delivers what the package promised
```

### Clarity and Flow
```
☐ Every sentence earns its place
☐ The wording is simple enough for an eighth grader to understand
☐ The post flows from one sentence to the next
☐ The post is easy to skim without forcing a line break after every sentence
☐ The post uses complete, connected sentences
☐ The draft contains no corporate buzzwords or filler
```

### Accuracy and Proof
```
☐ Every fact, claim and statistic has been verified
☐ Every important claim traces back to source material
☐ Approximate figures remain approximate
☐ The draft contains no invented connection between facts
☐ THE WRITER'S LEVEL OF CERTAINTY MATCHES THE EVIDENCE
```

### Voice and Pride
```
☐ The post sounds like a human speaking
☐ The post preserves the writer's real voice, opinions and experience
☐ The draft contains no generic AI phrases
☐ The post is free of spelling, grammar and punctuation mistakes
☐ You are proud to put your name behind it
```

### The read-aloud pass
> *"Read the final draft aloud. If you stumble, revise it. If a sentence sounds like something
> you would never say, revise it. If a claim cannot be proved, remove it or find the proof."*

# PART 6 · STEP 8 · Sustaining it `[DOC]`

**3–5 posts per week.** Five gives more ideas, hooks, formats and media to test. If five is too
heavy at the start, publish three for the first two or three weeks and increase when the
process feels comfortable.

> *"The calendar and CPIO worksheet should reduce the time each post takes because the major
> decisions happen before drafting."*

### Writing for yourself — the weekly rhythm
```
1  Review the buyer-question map
2  Choose next week's post ideas
3  Complete CPIO for each post
4  Draft in one or two focused sessions
5  Choose media
6  Run every draft through the editing checklist
7  Schedule or publish
8  Record performance and useful audience feedback
```

### Writing for a client — the interview method

**This is the ghostwriting delivery model, stated plainly. Recorded because it is the offer
Shubham is building toward.**

```
· A 45-MINUTE CALL EVERY TWO WEEKS
· Before the call, prepare TEN QUESTIONS:
      4 top-of-funnel
      4 middle-of-funnel
      2 bottom-of-funnel
  drawn from the content strategy, the buyer-question map, recent company events,
  and current market conversations
· Ask the founder the questions AS IF THEY ARE A GUEST ON A PODCAST
· LET THEM RAMBLE. It is encouraged.
· Use the recording and transcript as the source for TEN POSTS
· Complete CPIO before drafting each one
· Pair each post with media when media improves the idea
· Send all drafts in ONE document or content calendar
· Founder reviews in 15 MINUTES
· Ask for specific feedback on tone, voice, word choice, accuracy, missing context
· LOG THAT FEEDBACK so the next set needs fewer edits
```

> *"The founder's words, stories, examples, and decisions are what make the posts sound like
> the founder."*

**45 founder-minutes → 10 posts.** That is the unit economics of the offer.

# PART 7 · STEP 9 · The monthly review `[DOC]`

> *"Do not rebuild your strategy around one post. Look for patterns across the full month."*

### Record per post — 14 fields
```
topic · buyer question · funnel stage · hook type · format · media · word count
impressions · reactions · comments · reposts · profile visits (when available)
qualified comments or messages
calls, opportunities or revenue influenced by the post
```

### Then ask, literally — 8 questions
```
1  Which topics reached the right buyers?
2  Which buyer questions produced useful comments or messages?
3  Which hooks earned attention AND paid off their promise?
4  Which formats helped the reader understand the idea?
5  Which media choices improved the post?
6  Which posts produced profile visits, calls or sales conversations?
7  Which posts had HIGH REACH and LITTLE COMMERCIAL VALUE?
8  Which posts had MODEST REACH and CLEAR BUSINESS VALUE?
```

> *"Engagement gives you useful information. It does not tell the full story. Judge each post
> based on the job it was created to do."*

### Six actions from the review
```
· Keep topics that repeatedly reach the right audience
· Expand buyer questions that create useful discussion
· Add stronger examples or proof when an idea feels generic
· CHANGE THE PACKAGE when the idea is useful but the post does not earn attention
· Remove topics that do not serve the buyer or the business
· Keep testing formats and media — WITHOUT treating either as a cure for a weak idea
```
