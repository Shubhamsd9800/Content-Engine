## THE NICHE

> on the way.**

AI is how the work gets done, not what the account is about. It appears in almost

## ANTI-VISION

systems are real, and every one of them belongs to someone else. Still on a
monthly salary. Still asking permission for leave. Nothing published — all that
learning stayed in my head. No business of my own. No international clients.
Nobody who found me because of something I made.

## POST-MORTEM

**"I'll start posting once the system is ready."**

The system was never ready, because "ready" is not a state a system reaches. It is
a permission I kept declining to give myself.

Evidence: six of twelve ledger entries say *built, wired, complete, never run*. A
workflow that stopped at step 3 of 10. Sixty-seven content pieces written, zero
posted. One Instagram story that people actually asked about, never followed up.

## MISSION

**I want the things I build to be mine — and sold by me, to the people who need
them.** Business is the form that takes. Social media is how those people find out
I exist. Reels and LinkedIn are where the knowledge goes out.

Not the money. The money is a goal. This is what sits underneath it: ten years of
building systems that belong to someone else is the outcome I cannot accept.

*Internal compass, never a public title: I am the pathfinder. I go ahead and bring
back the part that works.*

## AUDIENCE

Mixed, deliberately.

**Consumed by:** anyone stuck trying to build or grow something in tech, AI or
business without a team behind them. **A state, not a job title** — the PEER
definition below governs. Students and engineers sit inside it; so do non-technical
people running a shop, a brand or a side business. What they share is being stuck,
not what they do for a living.

**The commercial target inside that:** the **corporate and small-business** side —
business owners, solo founders, and working professionals who need a digital thing
built and have no technical person. **Never consumer.**

They have tried an agency that ghosted them, a freelancer who disappeared, or an AI
tool that got them 60% there and stopped. At 11pm they search *"how much does a
website cost"*, *"can I build an app without a developer"*, *"why does my
AI-generated site look AI-generated."*

**The gap, and how it works:** whatever knowledge gap I notice, I become the bridge
for it. The gap I see today is that everyone teaching AI is teaching advanced
people — the people who do not know what Claude, Codex or an MCP even is are the
majority, and nobody is serving them. **Next month it will be a different gap.**
The bridging is the constant; the specific gap is not.

**Why the mixed audience works here:** one post can do both jobs. *"I wired five
tools into a workflow and never ran it — here is what those ten days cost."* An
engineer learns not to do it. A business owner learns that one person can build the
whole thing, and now knows who to call.

## TWO AUDIENCES, TWO OFFERS

The peers are not a leak. **They are the second revenue line.**

```
PEER  — someone trying to BUILD something and stuck.
        Defined by STATE, not by job title. Codes or doesn't; irrelevant.
        Often procrastinating on the build rather than blocked by it — the
        three-slide AI deck that felt like progress. I was this person.
        Wants the craft.
        Buys the PRODUCT — the engine, the workflow, templates, a guide.

BUYER — someone who has a BUSINESS PROBLEM and wants it gone.
        Wants the outcome, not the method. Corporate and small-business —
        never consumer.
        Buys the SERVICE:
          · web development         capability proven · no client yet
          · SaaS / MVP builds       capability proven · no client yet
          · AI automations          learning-stage
          · LinkedIn ghostwriting   testing — on my own account first
```

**These are often THE SAME PERSON at two different moments.** The peers are not a
leak and never were the supply side. A working professional trying to start a café,
a brand or a CRM is a peer on Monday and a buyer the week he runs out of evenings.

**Stage discipline applies to the offer list itself.** Nothing in it may be
presented publicly as a service already offered until it has shipped for somebody
else. Today that is zero of four. Naming what I build is allowed; naming what I
have delivered is not.

Same account, same content, two offers attached to two halves of one audience. This
is Koe's model — the brand is a traffic source; the offer is separate and can be
anything.

**Most creators run one or the other.** Tooling creators reach peers and sell
products. Outcome creators reach buyers and sell services. Running both is
deliberate here, not accidental.

**Constraints on it:**
- The ratio gets watched, never assumed. Every Brief records `AUDIENCE`.
- **One post, one reader.** Which reader is decided per Brief when the content idea
  arrives — not by a standing rule. Writing to both inside one piece is what makes
  content generic.
- **The product offer waits.** Peers buy a product; there is no product yet and zero
  posts. Post-20 decision.

## THE HOOK-END RULE — how READER is actually decided

The audience is a consequence of the **hook**, never of the topic. The same build
produces two different audiences depending on which end the post opens on.

> Cafe Management System, opened at the **craft** end —
> *"Here's the LangGraph architecture behind the café system."*
> → engineers. They admire it and go build their own. `READER = peer`
>
> Cafe Management System, opened at the **cost** end —
> *"A café owner loses money every month to manual billing. Here's what stopped it."*
> → operators. Some of them message. `READER = buyer`

Identical work. Identical proof. Different first eight words.

**So READER is not a property of the project — it is a property of the OPENING**,
and it is chosen deliberately per Brief. This is why `mixed` is illegal: a post
that opens at both ends opens at neither.

## HOW CLIENTS ACTUALLY ARRIVE

**Content is not the client channel. Outreach is. Content is the proof that makes
outreach work.**

When a cold email lands in September, they will search the name. What they find is
the difference between a cold email and a credible one. Peers who follow are not
wasted — they are what makes the asset look real to the buyer who checks.

## THE BULLSEYE — mix 3-2-2 per seven pieces

**RING 1 — three per seven**
- Resources I found and where. Skill marketplaces, UI inspiration, font libraries,
  cheap domains. The seven-source list.
- What I built and what broke. Named root cause, real fix.
- Systems I build and how they are wired.
- Getting AI to produce work that does not look AI-generated. The method.

**RING 2–3 — two per seven**
- The basics nobody is teaching, for people who do not know the tools exist.
- Beliefs I can argue — *"your job is a playground, not a safe place."*
- Web, SaaS, MVP work. What a business needs built, and what getting it wrong costs.

**RING 4 — two per seven**
- AI and industry news with my opinion attached. Claude down for hours. "4% of the
  world's code is now committed by Claude." Microsoft cuts 6,000 — here is what you
  do about it.
- Founders' journeys, told from the reader's current position.

**RING 5 — never**
- "AI is changing everything." Motivational quotes with nothing under them.

**A tradeoff recorded on purpose:** resource posts sit at ring 1 by decision, not by
accident. They are the most copyable content on the internet and the fastest route
to being found. **The building posts are the moat; the resource posts are the
reach.** Revisit at 20 posts with real numbers.

**Hook framing, from Koe:** frame broadly, open on pain or desire — *"if you want…"*
/ *"if you don't want…"*. Broad hook, narrow subject. Never open at the
practitioner's level of awareness.

## THE NOT-LIST

1. Anything about Capgemini, under any framing.
2. Anything not personally shipped, presented as proven.
3. Day-in-my-life vlogging. Office routine, lunch break, friends. **I already wrote
   the script for this. It stays unwritten.**
4. Dance reels, trend reels, chamak-chalo reels. Anything where the format is the
   content.
5. Reposting someone else's story with no angle. The angle does not have to be mine
   — **it has to be the reader's problem.** Not "Sharan failed and it's inspiring."
   Instead: "Sharan spent two years failing. You are eighteen months in. Here is the
   part of his road you are standing on right now."
