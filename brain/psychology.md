# psychology.md — why people stop, stay and trust

**Every writer reads this.** `playbook.md` says *what shape to use and how the words go inside
it*. This says *why the shape works* — which is the only way a writer adapts to a situation it has not seen.

---

# THE MASTER PRINCIPLE

> **Expectations** are what they think will happen. **Reality** is what happens.
> Reality beats expectations → they stay. Expectations beat reality → they leave.

The consequence is severe: **if your points are things the audience already knows, their
existing knowledge IS the expectation, and you cannot beat it.** A piece that restates
common knowledge is a structural loss, not a mediocre one.

That is why the kill rule exists. It is not taste.

---

# WHY THEY STOP

**The orienting response.** An involuntary reflex — the brain interrupts itself when a
pattern breaks. You do not argue someone into stopping; you trigger a reflex. The hook is
engineering, not persuasion.

**The freeze point.** The frame where the thumb hesitates. On a reel that is frame one.

**The awareness level — frame broad, land narrow.**

> *"Most people on social media aren't intentionally trying to learn."*

The hook is read by someone who did not come looking for the subject. So it opens on
**pain or desire** — some permutation of *"if you want…"* / *"if you don't want…"* — and
the body is as specific as it needs to be.

**Never open at the practitioner's level of awareness.** *"My chunk sizes were perfect, my
retrieval was garbage"* is written for someone already inside the problem. At zero
followers that is almost nobody, and the piece dies before the mechanics ever get read.

A large account can open at practitioner level, because it already has the practitioners.
**That is a downgrade case: the move works because of their audience, and audience does not
transfer.**

Broad hook. Narrow body. The hook earns the read; the body earns the trust.

**The 4 S's — all four in the first beat.**
Subject (they know what it's about) · Stakes (they know why it matters) ·
Speed (under ~8 words) · Super-clear (no cleverness that needs decoding).

**The triple hook.** Spoken, on-screen text and visual say the **same thing in different
words**. If they contradict, attention splits across three channels and dies before any
of them lands.

**Click confirmation.** The first lines confirm — and ideally beat — the expectation the
hook set. Meeting it holds them. Exceeding it hooks them.

**Relatability before credentials.** Open on what they recognise. Never on who you are.

## Hook modifiers — the delivery layer

Layered **on top of** a hook that already works. **A modifier cannot rescue a weak hook.**

The mechanism they all serve: *the number one reason someone scrolls off is that they
subconsciously deem you not worth their time.* So the hook's job is not "be interesting" —
it is **produce evidence in under three seconds that the next forty seconds are worth it.**

| Modifier | Mechanism | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **"In [n] seconds"** | converts an open-ended ask into a bounded one | live |
| **On-screen countdown** | makes the bound visual; the falling number is an open loop | live, sparingly |
| **Objection handling** — *"I know what you're thinking…"* then name their doubt | pre-empts the thought that causes the scroll. Being understood is proof you know the domain. | **live — highest value** |
| **Beat drop in the first 15s**, on the reveal line | audio punctuation on the payoff | live — free, an edit decision |
| **Visual / numerical social proof** | answers "worth my time" with evidence | **not available** — no real number exists yet |
| **Physical open loop** (start a task you finish by the end) | retention from the task, not the point | **not used** — a gimmick cannot carry a technical claim |

Write the hook with the content rules first. *Then* ask whether one modifier makes
committing cheaper. **Never more than two on one hook** — three is a magic trick and
signals thin content. Shipping without one is the common case.

# WHY THEY STAY

**Dopamine is prediction, not pleasure.** You are not rewarding the viewer, you are making
them *anticipate*. The moment they can predict the ending they leave, because the
anticipation was the thing holding them.

**Vending machine vs slot machine.** A piece that delivers exactly what was promised,
immediately and completely, is a vending machine: correct, and unwatched.

**Zeigarnik effect** — unfinished tasks stay in memory better than completed ones. This is
why a fully-closed piece is *forgotten*, not merely dull. **Leave one thing unclosed.**

**Second-best point first.** Lead with your second-best point, then your best, then
descending. Two things where the second is better creates a pattern that value is
*rising*, so they stay for the third. Best first makes the second a step down and trains
them that value is decaying.

**The value loop — every body point, in this order.**
1. **Context** — what it is, simply.
2. **Application** — how to do it, with concrete examples.
3. **Framing** — why it matters and how it fits the whole.

Framing is the part everyone skips and the part that makes them stay.

**Rehook between every two points.** *"That mattered. But without the next one it doesn't
hold."* Interest spikes then decays — rehook before it bottoms out.

**Zoom in, zoom out.** Big picture → tactic → big picture. A wave, not a flat line.

**Data → emotion → data.** Never a continuous block of facts. An audience fed pure data
settles onto one flat emotional level and leaves. Emotion is not decoration on top of
information; it is the mechanism by which information keeps being received.

**Time under attention.** The metric is total minutes a person spends with your thinking,
not reach. One person for forty minutes beats four hundred for three seconds.

# WHY THEY TRUST YOU

## The trust matrix — three legs, each fails alone

| Leg | Does | Alone it fails as |
|---|---|---|
| **Attraction** | gets the right people to look | they came for a format, not you |
| **Authenticity** | expresses what you actually believe | they like you but wouldn't pay you |
| **Authority** | demonstrates you can do the thing | you're a search result, interchangeable |

**One post serves one leg.** A post serving all three serves none. Name the leg before
writing.

## Authentic polarization

> If you're liked by everybody, you're liked by nobody.

Content nobody can disagree with is content nobody remembers. State a belief clearly
enough to be argued with. Disagreement is engagement *and* it filters the audience down
to people who convert.

**Polarize on technical and craft questions only** — where there are actual grounds.
Manufactured takes on business or life philosophy read as posturing from someone 1.5 years
in, and destroy the authority leg while trying to serve the authenticity one.

## The contrarian intro — polarization as structure

1. State the **common belief** first. They hold it; restating it says "this is about you"
   and builds the ground the contrast stands on.
2. **Then contradict it.** The contrast is what stuns them into listening.

You cannot build contrast without stating the belief first. The order is fixed.

## Peak-end rule

People judge an experience by its most intense moment and its ending, not its average.
This is why the last ten seconds carry disproportionate weight for likes, shares and
rewatches. It also means: **engineer one peak.** Uniform quality scores worse than an
uneven piece with a real high point.

**The outro.** Summarise, remind them of the pain that got solved, leave on a high note.
The free chocolate with the restaurant bill.

## Pratfall effect — and the warning inside it

A visible, non-fatal mistake increases likeability — **but only for someone already seen
as competent.** For an unknown it inverts: the mistake reads as incompetence.

**So a failure post must carry the competence in the same piece** — the root cause, the
fix, the proof. Never the failure alone. Never the failure as the punchline.

## Confidence downgrade

A structure borrowed from a large account carries **that account's authority**, and
authority does not transfer.

> "Here's why everyone is wrong about X" → **"Here's what broke when I tried X."**

An engineering audience detects borrowed posture instantly, and once detected the
authority leg is gone for good.

## Personality over product; and the villain

People follow people. **The specific detail is the personality** — the two days lost, the
token count that looked fine. "Best practices for chunking" is the product, and products
are interchangeable.

Every strong position is **against** something. Not a person — a practice, a default, an
assumption inherited without checking.

# STORYTELLING TECHNIQUE

**Specificity beats polish.** A rough sentence with a real number outperforms a
well-turned one without. This is permission to ship rough.

**Story before strategy.** The story is the vehicle; the point rides on it. Not the
reverse.

**Don't summarise — zoom into the moment.** The difference between "the retrieval was
broken for two days" and a scene. Five ways in:
**location** (where you were) · **actions** (what you were doing) ·
**thoughts** (raw and unfiltered, what you actually thought) ·
**emotions** (shown, never stated) · **dialogue** (the exact words said).

**Context, then conflict.** Establish the situation with real detail before making a
claim. Then the tension inside it — what is breaking, what is at stake.

**Questions planted forward.** "What happens next?" — keep them predicting ahead of the
piece.

**Curse of knowledge.** You cannot un-know what you know, so you systematically
overestimate what the reader knows. Fix: name the thing a beginner would Google.

# THE CTA

**At zero followers the CTA drives conversation or return** — a question they can answer
**from their own experience**, or an explicit next part. Not a question testing whether
they read the post.

**Native embed.** Decide during the outline which point naturally sets up your resource as
the solve. Frame the pain, offer it, move on. If it feels like an ad break, it is one.

"DM me to build your website" at zero followers is shouting in an empty room, and it
signals desperation to the handful who see it.

# WHERE IDEAS COME FROM

**"I have no ideas" is an articulation problem, not an input problem.** Everything becomes
a source once you can articulate. Train articulation, not idea-hunting.

**Write every day.** Writing is not the output of thinking — it *is* the thinking.
**Capture immediately.** An uncaptured idea is a lost idea.
**Study 20–30 pieces from creators you admire** — for the articulation, not the content.
Tear them down with `creator-analyst`; do not just read them.
