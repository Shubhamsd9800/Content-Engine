# §1 · WHERE POSTS COME FROM

His system mines ideas from sales call transcripts, support tickets, objections and lost
deals. **You have no clients, so that source is empty.** Until it fills, ideas come from three
places, and all three are real.

| # | Bucket | Source on disk | Funnel | The test it must pass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **BUILT** | `brain/ledger.md` — 12 built-for-self entries | MOF | Did you build the thing, and can you show it? |
| **2** | **STUCK** | Your own stuck-points, past and present | MOF | Were you actually stuck on this, and did you get out? |
| **3** | **MOVING** | What is happening in AI and building this week | TOF | Do you have a read on it that the reader does not already have? |

**Bucket 3 is not a lesser bucket.** Timely industry topics are a named top-of-funnel bucket in
the source. The reader who has not seen the news is served by someone closer to the tools than
they are. That is the account.

> **⊘ ADAPTED — the BOF bucket is switched off.** Case studies, testimonials and customer
> results have no material behind them. `BUILT, NOT DELIVERED` is a standing rule and the
> accuracy rules forbid inventing the rest. **Run TOF and MOF only.** BOF switches on with the
> first shipped client project.

## The four-question test — every idea, before it becomes a Brief

```
1  Does my reader already care about this question?
2  Do I have something useful and specific to say about it?
3  Can I support the answer with a real example, result, process, or opinion?
4  Will the answer help the reader make a decision or get a result?
```

**Fail any one and the idea is not ready.** Go and find more — do not fill the gap with a
claim you cannot back. That is the accuracy rule and it is not negotiable.

## The commentary rule — for bucket 3 specifically

A screenshot plus a reaction is not a post. **A screenshot plus your read is.**

```
✗  "OpenAI made X free. Big for India."          ← a reaction. Everyone has one.
✓  "X is free now. Here is what actually changes
    if you are building on it."                   ← commentary. You have one, they don't.
```

The brand promise is the check: **who is stuck, and what do they leave with?** If the reader
leaves with only the news they already saw, the post failed before it was written.

# §2 · CPIO — the four decisions before you draft

**Complete all four in sequence. Do not write a line until each is clear.**

This is not a mental exercise. **The worksheet gets filled in, in writing, per post.** That is
how the source runs it commercially, and it is the reason his writers never face a blank page.

> **CPIO is its own stage.** Brief → CPIO worksheet → draft. The filled worksheet is
> inspectable before any prose exists, which is the whole point.

---

## C · CONVEY — what this post must communicate

**One thing. Nothing more.** Every sentence in the post serves that one point. If a line does
not support it, cut the line.

Decide which it is: **a story · an opinion · a framework · a process · a case study.**

Answer three questions:

```
1  What should the reader understand after reading this?
2  What part of the source material supports that idea?
3  What result should this post create?
```

**Question 3 takes a literal answer.** "Build trust with founders who need an MVP." "Create
discussion around a real opinion about AI tooling." "Explain a process someone asked me about."

**Banned as an answer: "build authority", "thought leadership", "grow the account."** Those are
not results, they are hopes.

> **If you cannot state the purpose in one sentence, go back to the source material.**
> Do not invent a stronger opinion, lesson or conclusion than the source supports.

```
CONVEY:  [one sentence — the exact purpose of this post]
```

---

## P · PACKAGE — how the idea gets presented

The source calls this the most important of the four. Packaging controls **angle, format, hook
and structure** together.

### Pick the package from what the idea is

| The idea is | The package |
|---|---|
| A story | Story hook + a photo of you |
| A framework | Desired-result hook + a worksheet or visual |
| A long list of useful points | Carousel |
| Industry commentary | Headline / tweet / post screenshot + your read |
| A process | Result-led hook + a clear breakdown of the steps |
| A dense process | Infographic or cheat sheet |
| A case study | Result → starting point → process → proof *(off until clients exist)* |

**Do not force a format because it is familiar or easy to write.**

### The hook — one job

**Give the right reader a reason to press "see more."** Nothing else.

You do that by opening a **curiosity gap**: the space between what the reader knows now and
what they must keep reading to learn. Give enough to see the value. **Keep the answer for the
body.**

> **The hook makes a promise and the body pays it off.** An unpaid hook loses the reader for
> good — and it is the first thing the editing checklist tests.

**Eight elements a strong hook uses — one or more:**

```
1  A specific result                    5  The start of a story
2  Relevant authority                   6  A process the reader wants to understand
3  A clear desired outcome              7  A strong opinion you can support
4  A real problem                       8  A useful piece of information left unresolved
```

**Three curiosity-gap mechanisms — name the one you used:**

| # | Mechanism | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Pair two things that shouldn't go together** | *"I [do X at scale], but I've never [obvious prerequisite]."* |
| **2** | **Reveal the decision, hold back the reason** | *"Last month I [surprising decision]."* — and stop. |
| **3** | **Start at the moment something changed** | *"Ten minutes into [situation], [the thing that broke]."* |

**Three hard rules:**
```
✗  Never invent a number, result, timeframe or claim because it improves the hook
✗  Never create false suspense
✗  Never reveal the full insight in the opening
```

### ⊘ ADAPTED · THE PROOF SUBSTITUTION — read this before writing any hook

**Element 2, "relevant authority", is real and it works.** The source's own hooks run on it:
*"5,000 posts, 50 founders, backed by YC, a16z and Accel."* Specific, checkable, earned.

**You do not have that, and two independent sources now agree that the credential hook is how
this genre opens.** So this is the single most likely way a draft goes wrong: it reaches for a
credential, finds none, and either inflates or deflates.

**The substitution is permanent, not a temporary workaround:**

```
✗  CREDENTIAL HOOK   authority borrowed from scale, tenure, headcount or revenue
                     "I've built X for Y companies over Z years"
                     → unavailable. 1.5 years. No employer named. No clients.
                       Money numbers are off limits.

✓  ARTIFACT HOOK     authority carried by the thing itself
                     the build, the breakage, the specific decision, the real number
                     that came out of work you actually did
                     → available today. Needs no follower count and no tenure.
```

**Worked substitutions:**

```
✗  "After building AI systems for years, here's what I've learned about RAG."
✓  "My retrieval was returning garbage for two days. The chunk size was fine.
    The problem was three lines above it."

✗  "I help founders ship faster."
✓  "I rebuilt the same onboarding flow four times before I understood why it kept breaking."
```

**The specificity rule survives intact. Only the source of authority changes** — from *who I
am* to *what I built*. That is also the register `playbook.md` found independently in S01, and
it is the only credibility available at 1.5 years that does not posture.

**Three things that never enter a hook or a post:**
```
✗  An employer name, in any framing
✗  Any money number — rates, income, earnings, client value
✗  Anything not personally shipped, presented as proven
```

### Media — part of the package, decided here

| Use | When |
|---|---|
| A photo of you | personal story, founder experience, anything vulnerable |
| A screenshot, internal doc or visual | framework, process, checklist, a real result |
| Video | when hearing you explain it builds trust the text cannot |
| Carousel | a long sequence or list that genuinely benefits from pages |
| Headline or post screenshot | industry commentary |
| **Text alone** | **when no media improves the idea** |

> **Media improves a strong idea. It never rescues a weak one.**
> A post reaching for media to compensate is a post whose CONVEY was thin.

```
PACKAGE:       [the format]
ANGLE:         [how the idea is presented]
HOOK POSTURE:  [what the hook leads with · what stays unresolved · which mechanism]
MEDIA:         [what, or "text alone"]
```

## I · INFORMATION — what goes in, and explicitly what stays out

Two questions, and the second matters as much as the first:

```
What does this post NEED to make its point?
What does it NOT need?
```

Do not carry every detail from the source. Keep only what improves **the argument, the story,
clarity, evidence, or reader value.**

> **Every sentence fights for its life and earns its place.**
> The full idea, in the fewest words that keep its meaning.

**The accuracy rules — these are the ones that protect the account:**

```
· Verify every specific claim before it goes in
· Never fabricate a statistic, date, timeframe or performance number
· If the source figure is approximate, KEEP IT APPROXIMATE
  "about two days" beats an invented "16 hours" — and a real number beats both
· If information you need is missing, FLAG IT. Do not guess.
· Every tool named carries its real stage: found · installed · testing · shipped.
  A stage is never skipped upward.
```

```
REQUIRED:   [facts, stories, examples, context this post needs]
EXCLUDE:    [related material that would weaken the focus]
MISSING:    [what is needed to write this accurately — "None" when complete]
```

## O · ORDER — the flow, built for a cold reader

**Assume the reader does not know who you are, what you do, or how you could help them.** They
were served this post by an algorithm, mid-scroll, having asked for nothing.

Order the information the way the reader meets the problem — not the way you discovered it.

| Role | Its job |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | establish value, earn the click |
| **Setup** | enough context to understand the topic |
| **Development** | the idea, in the order that makes it easiest to follow |
| **Support** | evidence and examples, placed where they strengthen the point being made |
| **Ending** | complete the idea |

**Three closing rules:**
```
✗  Do not repeat the conclusion in different words
✗  Do not add a CTA unless the Brief calls for one
✓  The ending completes the idea. It does not summarise it.
```

> **⊘ THE CTA RULE — this settles a contradiction that was open in the engine.**
> `linkedin-writer` v1 STEP 5 hardcoded *"conversation or return only."* `script-writer` §5
> said something adjacent but different. **Neither is right, because the CTA is not the
> writer's decision.** The source's rule is better: *do not add a CTA unless the source,
> strategy or brief calls for one.* **The Brief decides. The writer obeys.** No CTA is a valid
> and common outcome.

**Then run the cold-reader check.** Read it back as a stranger. Does the order hold? If a
section only makes sense because you already knew the ending, reorder it.

```
HOOK:         [what the opening must establish]
SETUP:        [what context comes next]
DEVELOPMENT:  [how the idea develops]
SUPPORT:      [where evidence and examples sit]
ENDING:       [how it finishes]
```

**Only now do you write.** The blank page is already dead — drafting from a completed CPIO
worksheet is assembly, not invention.

# §3 · DRAFTING

**Write for a cold audience.** Every time. The algorithm serves you to people sporadically, so
each post carries its own context. Not your life story — just enough for the reader to see why
the idea matters and why you are the one saying it.

**Write how you speak.** If you would not say *utilize* out loud, do not write it. Say *use*.
Shortest simplest word that carries the idea. **Read the post aloud before it goes out.** If it
sounds awkward spoken, it is wrong.

**Clarity beats cleverness.** Simple language, plain analogies, active voice. One idea per
sentence.

**Make every sentence flow into the next.** If you have to reread to see how two thoughts
connect, so will they. And:

> **Do not use repeated openings, stacked fragments or dramatic cadence to fake momentum.
> The logic creates the momentum.**

**Format for mobile.**
```
· You do NOT need a line break after every sentence
· Line breaks separate IDEAS AND ARGUMENTS — not individual lines
· No block longer than about four lines on a phone
· Bullets or numbers when the reader must hold several distinct points
· No special characters faking bold or underline
```

**Cut everything that does not matter.** *very · really · quite · just* — gone when they add
nothing. Any sentence that does not teach, support the point, improve the story, or set up
what comes next — gone.

> **A post can be long when the idea needs the detail. It is never longer than the idea
> requires.**

## Language and length

```
LANGUAGE   English. Always. No exception on LinkedIn.
           (Hinglish is the Instagram spoken default — see brain/voice.md.)

LENGTH     Set by the idea, not by a target.
           Reference points, not rules: the fold sits around 210 characters;
           1,200–1,500 characters is a common working range on the platform.
           Neither is a quota. A short post with a complete idea beats a padded one.
```

# §4 · THE GATE — run every post through this

**Do not skip it.** The source is blunt about this and he is right: the checklist is what
separates a draft from a post.

### Point and Payoff
```
☐ The post makes ONE clear point
☐ The body fully pays off the promise the hook made
☐ Every part supports the CONVEY line
☐ The ending completes the idea without repeating it
```

### Context and Usefulness
```
☐ A cold reader has the context to understand it
☐ The reader gets a useful takeaway
☐ Examples and explanations are specific enough to use
☐ Related detail that weakened the focus has been removed
```

### Hook and Package
```
☐ The hook gives the right reader a clear reason to press "see more"
☐ The interest it creates is honest
☐ Format and media fit the idea
☐ NO invented number, result or claim in the hook
☐ The post delivers what the package promised
```

### Clarity and Flow
```
☐ Every sentence earns its place
☐ Simple enough for an eighth grader
☐ It flows sentence to sentence
☐ Skimmable WITHOUT a line break after every sentence
☐ Complete, connected sentences
☐ No corporate buzzwords, no filler
```

### Accuracy and Proof
```
☐ Every fact, claim and statistic verified
☐ Every important claim traces to real source material
☐ Approximate figures stayed approximate
☐ No invented connection between facts
☐ THE LEVEL OF CERTAINTY MATCHES THE EVIDENCE
☐ Every tool named carries its real stage
☐ Nothing implies a client delivery
```

### Voice and Pride
```
☐ It sounds like a human speaking
☐ It preserves your real voice, opinions and experience
☐ No generic AI phrases
☐ No spelling, grammar or punctuation mistakes
☐ You are proud to put your name on it
```

**Then read it aloud.** Stumble → revise. Sounds like something you would never say → revise.
Claim you cannot prove → remove it or find the proof.

> **The checklist improves a post. It cannot kill one.**
> That is what `script-doctor` is for, and **a kill verdict there is a successful run.**
> Both instruments, in that order. Never one instead of the other.

**Rules 1–16 are the source's prohibited-patterns list.** Delete before publishing.

```
 1  Reversal framing
 2  A common belief followed by a dramatic correction
 3  Rhetorical questions
 4  Repeated sentence openings used to create rhythm
 5  Stacked sentence fragments
 6  Broad claims beginning with "Most people"
 7  Abstract comparisons with no concrete effect
 8  Corporate buzzwords
 9  Cliche openings
10  Unnecessary adverbs
11  Artificial symmetry
12  Dramatic cadence
13  Forced summaries
14  Forced CTAs
15  Em dashes
16  Any phrase on the banned list in brain/voice.md
```

**Rules 17–24 come from the engine, and they outrank everything above.**

```
17  Never an employer name. In any framing. Ever.
18  Never a money number — rates, income, earnings, client value
19  Never imply a client delivery. BUILT, NOT DELIVERED. Every ledger entry is
    built-for-self and there are zero clients.
20  Never present anything not personally shipped as proven
21  Never politics or religion
22  Never named criticism of a person, creator or company.
    (This fences the source's Case Study format: the praise half survives,
     the "what everyone else does wrong" half does not.)
23  Never motivational content as a FORM — a quote with nothing under it.
    A story with a real number and a named cost is not motivational content.
24  If a draft could have been written by anyone about anything, it fails.
    DELETE IT AND START AGAIN. Do not polish it.
```

## THE MONTHLY REVIEW — the half that works today

Record per post: **topic · bucket · funnel stage · hook mechanism · package · media · length ·
impressions · reactions · comments · reposts · profile visits.**

Then ask, across a full month — never off one post:

```
1  Which topics reached the right reader?
2  Which ideas produced useful comments, not just reactions?
3  Which hooks earned attention AND paid off their promise?
4  Which packages helped the reader understand the idea?
5  Which posts had HIGH REACH and LITTLE VALUE?
6  Which posts had MODEST REACH and CLEAR VALUE?
```

> **Reach does not decide the value of a post. Judge each post by the job it was created to
> do.** A post that reaches 200 of the right people beats one that reaches 20,000 of the wrong
> ones.

**Six actions from the review:** keep topics that reach the right reader · expand ideas that
create real discussion · add stronger proof where an idea reads generic · **change the package
when the idea is good but the post did not earn attention** · drop topics that serve nobody ·
keep testing formats — **without treating a format as a cure for a weak idea.**

Results feed `work/log/published.md` and `voice.md` OBSERVED.
