## Audience

Two readers, one account. **Never both inside one piece.**

**PEER** — someone trying to build something and **stuck**. Defined by state, not job title.
Codes or doesn't; irrelevant. Often procrastinating on the build rather than blocked by it.
Wants the craft. Buys the **product** — deferred until post 20.

**BUYER** — someone with a business problem who wants it gone. Wants the outcome, not the
method. Corporate and small-business, **never consumer**. Has been ghosted by an agency, or
got 60% there with an AI tool and stopped. Buys the **service**: web development, SaaS and
MVP builds.

**Often the same person at two moments.** A working professional starting a café is a peer
on Monday and a buyer the week he runs out of evenings.

Which reader arrives is decided by the **opening**, not the topic — see THE HOOK-END RULE in
`niche.md`. `mixed` and `unknown` are illegal values.

## Point of view

> **Teaching is aimed above people's heads. Everyone else stays stuck learning, and never
> ships anything.**

AI is today's example of it, not the belief itself. `niche.md` records that the specific gap
changes month to month; **the belief does not**, which is why it is phrased without naming a
technology.

**Evidence it is his and not borrowed:** `ledger.md` holds twelve entries and six read
*built, wired, complete, never run*. Sixty-seven content pieces written, zero posted. Day 27,
still zero.

**What it rejects.** An idea written for people already inside the problem. *"Advanced RAG
chunking strategies"* fails it. *"What an MCP actually is, if you have never installed one"*
passes — that piece IS the point of view in action.

**Who argues back:** plenty of developers hold that you must keep learning or fall behind.
That disagreement is what makes it worth saying.

## Brand promise

> **"He starts where I'm stuck, and I always leave with something I can use."**

The thought a reader should hold after three pieces. It pays off the point of view directly:
the complaint is teaching pitched too high, and the promise is that this account is not that.

**It is also a check `brief-builder` can run.** Who is stuck, and what do they leave with? If
either answer is blank, the piece does not serve the promise.

The mechanism is rule **R-1** — an artifact handed over beats news reported.

*Rejected at interview: "he found it first and handed it over" — the promise every resource
account already makes, and someone always finds it faster. Kept as the mechanism, not the
promise.*

## Off limits

**From the engine NOT-LIST, unchanged.** Capgemini under any framing · anything not
personally shipped, presented as proven · any claim of delivered client work while zero
clients exist · day-in-my-life vlogging · dance, trend and chamak-chalo reels · reposting
someone's story with no angle · ring 5 · any post whose `READER` is `mixed` or `unknown`.

**Added by interview, Day 27:**

- **Politics and religion.** Nothing, in any register.
- **Money numbers.** No income screenshots, no rates, no earnings claims. The commercial
  target is business owners; income content attracts peers who want the revenue, not buyers
  who want work done.
- **Named criticism.** No calling out specific people, creators or companies. Attack ideas,
  never names.

**Explicitly NOT banned — personal life.** The positioning is *one person building*. If the
person is invisible, the account is a resource feed. The evening a build cost, the thing that
broke at 1am, the last year and what it actually cost — all publishable.

**The fence that governs it — playbook S08, locked Day 26.** Polarize on **technical and
craft questions only**.

> ✅ *"Here's what the last year actually cost me"* — a story, specific, a reader recognises
>    himself in it.
> ❌ *"Here's what life has taught me about discipline"* — life philosophy at 1.5 years.
>    Reads as posturing and permanently damages the authority leg.

Same material. The first is owned. The second is not yet earned.

**Motivational content is not banned as a subject — it is banned as a FORM.** Ring 5 is
*"motivational quotes with nothing under them."* A quote card fails. A story with a real
number and a named cost passes, because the ANGLE RULE already permits *a real cost, named*.
